# Computational Materials Science

Computational materials science bridges fundamental thermodynamic theory and continuum mechanics to predict how materials behave under processing, deformation, and thermal regimes. My work centers on developing multi-scale modeling frameworks that capture microstructural evolution and mechanical response across relevant length and time scales.

## Core Research Domains

* **Mesoscale Microstructure Modeling:** Simulating grain growth, recrystallization, and phase transformation phenomena where continuum assumptions hold, but atomistic details are homogenized.
* **Microtexture & Anisotropy:** Tracking crystallographic orientation distributions and local misorientations to predict macroscale mechanical anisotropy and texture development during processing.
* **Thermodynamic-Kinetic Integration:** Coupling phenomenological kinetic laws with CALPHAD-based thermodynamic databases to model non-equilibrium phase transformations.

## Methodological Philosophy

Traditional continuum mechanics often treats materials as homogeneous media. By incorporating explicit microstructural features—such as grain boundaries, second-phase precipitates, and local crystallographic orientations—computational materials science enables:

1. **Predictive Processing:** Replacing empirical trial-and-error heat treatments with physics-based kinetic simulations.
2. **Structure-Property Linkage:** Quantitative mapping from processing parameters to synthetic microstructures and subsequent yield/failure criteria.
3. **Defect Tolerance:** Assessing the impact of local stress concentrations near phase boundaries and triple junctions.

!!! tip "Key Synergies"
    Our computational frameworks feed directly into high-performance numerical solvers and high-dimensional statistical analysis for data-driven discovery.
